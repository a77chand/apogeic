Apogeic 11

Code architecture, game workflow and mechanics · NASA Space Apps 2026 · Godot 4.7 (GDScript), exported to the web

Apogeic 11 teaches how real space missions are designed by letting the player make the trade-offs themselves: mass against power, data against money, speed against safety. The player trains on four puzzles, then flies twenty missions based on real spaceflights, and can take on a NASA problem about robots that must see in the long shadows at the Moon's south pole.

1. Game workflow

The main path runs left to right and top to bottom. Dashed arrows are loops: the player goes back to improve the design. Side activities (left, purple) can be opened from the title screen at any time.

2. Code architecture

The game is built entirely in code (no hand-made scenes besides main.tscn). It is split into layers. Each layer only talks to the layer below it, so the rules can be tested without any screen.

How one action flows through the layers

  • The player fits a part in the workshop: workshop.gd calls GameState.set_slot().
  • GameState updates design and emits design_changed.
  • The workshop refreshes: GameState.evaluate() → Sim.evaluate_design() gives mass, power, battery, data, cost and launch day; Sim.check_requirements() turns that into the six checks.
  • The screen draws the six meters and the readiness percentage from those results. No numbers are worked out in the screen itself.

4. Mechanics

4.1 Training puzzles

P1 Mass. Pack a photo satellite into a 3.6 kg rocket slot. Mass, money ($380k), power and data rate must all work at once; only one set of parts fits. The rocket's open payload bay shows each part as a block. Launching too heavy plays the break-up film.
P2 Power. Size panels and battery so the battery stays above 10% through a 35-minute eclipse and refills before the next one, within 0.7 kg. The film plays as soon as the puzzle opens.
P3 Data link. The camera makes more than the radio can send. Nine ideas: some work, some are traps, some only work together. The pipe shows the bottleneck. Spare mass, money and power limit the answer; the cleverest one is free.
P4 Budget and time. Plan build, test and licence on a timeline so the satellite is ready for launch day without running out of money, then play the year with surprises.

4.2 The six checks (requirements)

IdCheckHard?If it fails
R1Total mass at most the rocket slot (1.33 / 4 / 8 kg by CubeSat size)YesThe rocket refuses the satellite.
R2Deliver at least the weekly data goalNoFewer photos reach the ground.
R3Keep at least 10% spare powerNoThe battery drains a little every week.
R4Keep at least 10% of the budget in reserveNoA surprise cost can cancel the mission.
R5Battery stays above 10% in eclipseNoBrown-outs lose data; with no battery the satellite dies.
R6Ready to launch by the deadline dayNoThe launch slot is missed and rebooking costs money.

Readiness is the share of checks that pass. The rocket needs at least the level's minimum (80% in Earth Imager) to fly.

4.3 Workshop

  • Design values (Sim.evaluate_design): mass = bus + parts; power made by panels (capped by the craft in Earth Imager: 1U 2.5 W, 3U 20 W, 6U 57.6 W); power used by bus and parts; battery need = power used × eclipse minutes; data delivered = the smaller of made and sent.
  • The clock: one day passes every few seconds. Every day adds staff cost, and launch day = build day + post-build days + retests, never earlier than the licence.
  • Pressure: removing or swapping a fitted part costs rework days and money; a HURRY warning flashes when the launch date nears the deadline.
  • Back asks first: progress for this round is lost and leaving costs 1 life. "Bring back my last parts" can restore the design.
  • Each mission is its own puzzle: it offers only the spacecraft that really flew (CubeSat sizes where the mission used CubeSats, otherwise one satellite with no size choice) and only the parts that make sense for it, so each mission has a different best design (checked by tests/mission_check.gd). Earth Imager offers 1U, 3U and 6U with the team's values sheet: mass 1.33 / 4 / 8 kg, power 2.5 / 20 / 57.6 W, and a data goal that follows the data rate (19.6 / 125 / 125 kbps).

4.4 Test lab and launch

  • Test events (vibration, thermal, software …) give a choice with a 30-second timer. Leaving a problem unfixed records a flaw.
  • Launch outcome is decided by the player's choices, never by chance (Sim.launch_outcome):
    • On the pad: readiness below the minimum, so the launch is called off.
    • Max-Q (35% of the climb): heavy craft (above 80% of the limit) with a flaw, or a cracked antenna mount with the big antenna fitted.
    • Second stage (65%): above 95% of the mass limit with a check still failing.
    • Separation (100%): two or more flaws, or large panels with a flaw.
  • The rocket fails at the earliest stage that has a problem; the error code shown is random, the reasons are not.

4.5 Operations (one week at a time)

  • Data: made → stored → sent (radio limit); anything above on-board storage is lost. Low-res photos make less; an extra ground pass sends more for a fee and a cooldown.
  • Battery: safe mode recharges; negative spare power drains it; a weak battery causes brown-outs that lose a share of the data.
  • Fuel burns every week, faster for heavier craft. Events (solar storm, space junk …) appear at set weeks with a timer.
  • The mission ends when the weeks run out (complete), or early: battery empty, out of money, out of fuel, or orbit decay.

4.6 Stars and grade

  • ★ Goal reached: delivered data ≥ the mission goal.
  • ★ All checks passed at launch.
  • ★ Finished with reserve: completed with at least 10% of the budget left.
  • Grade: success (goal met), partial (60% of the goal or more), failed (less).

4.7 NASA problem: can the rover see? (a hopping rover)

  • 1 Sensors: choose sensors within mass, power and cost limits; a drive test (1x or 2x speed) shows the rover hopping over the rocks it sees and hitting the ones it cannot.
  • 2 Route (3 sets, each trickier): move with WASD or the arrow keys (or click squares) to plan a path for a solar-powered scout rover; shadows drain the battery, dust hides rocks, ridges block the way.
  • 3 Shadows (3 sets, each trickier): the same, but the Sun moves each step so shadows sweep across the map; waiting in sunlight recharges. Each challenge has its own tips and a Next button, and opens when the one before is done.

5. Progress, rewards and saving

  • Missions come in stages of 7. A stage opens when the stage before has 17 of its 21 stars. Missions open only after the four puzzles.
  • Lives: 5. A 0-star mission costs one, and so does leaving a mission part-way (Home, Quit or the workshop Back button warn first). One comes back every hour, or can be bought for 1,500 star dust.
  • Star dust: 500 the first time a mission gets 3 stars; 50 per right trivia answer (up to 3 rounds a day, 10 minutes apart).
  • Saved to user://save.json: puzzles and missions done, stars, lives, star dust, profile, settings (sound, music, text size, tips), truck and trivia progress.

6. Art, sound and fallbacks

  • Missing art never breaks a screen: UI.picture() shows the team's PNG if it exists, otherwise a code drawing from sprites.gd, otherwise a labelled box.
  • Team drawings in use: part icons, the six check icons, the three CubeSat bodies, UHF and S-band antennas, the dish, the ground station, Moon, Sun and night-side Earth, the 19-frame rocket animation, and the Mission Completed / Failed titles.
  • Sound effects and music are generated in code (no audio files), each with its own volume and mute.

7. Testing and extending

  • Run all tests headless: godot --headless --path . -s res://tests/run_tests.gd (sim, craft, progress, trivia and truck suites, plus load and mission checks).
  • Add a screen: write scripts/screens/name.gd and add one line to SCREENS in main.gd.
  • Add a mission: add data/levelN.json (or generate it with tools/make_missions.py); the mission list picks it up.
  • Add art: drop a PNG with the expected name into art/; it replaces the code drawing automatically.

Numbers marked as placeholders in the data files (part prices, battery sizes, event effects) are for gameplay and are labelled in the game. Sources are listed in the in-game Resources panel and in docs/CREDITS.md.
